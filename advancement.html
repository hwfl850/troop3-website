Advancement

Scout to Eagle, one rank at a time

Advancement is one of the eight methods of Scouting, not the point of it. You earn ranks by doing things, getting them signed off, and sitting a board of review. Here is how that works in Troop 3.

The ranks

Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class and First Class are about skills — camping, cooking, first aid, knots, swimming, navigation. Most Scouts reach First Class in their first twelve to eighteen months if they camp regularly. Star, Life and Eagle are about merit badges, service, and holding a position of responsibility.

Requirements can be worked on in any order and several at once, but each rank must be earned in sequence. Nothing is retroactive: the position of responsibility you held before you made Star does not count toward Star.

Getting things signed off

Bring your handbook to every meeting and every campout. Requirements are signed by the Scoutmaster, an assistant Scoutmaster, or a Scout the Scoutmaster has designated — usually a Troop Guide or Instructor. Once it is signed, it is done. It cannot be un-signed later because someone thinks you have forgotten it.

Everything also goes into Scoutbook, but your handbook is the record. If the two disagree, the handbook wins.

Merit badges and blue cards

  1. Pick a badge and ask the Scoutmaster for a blue card. Do this before you start work.
  2. Get the name of a registered counsellor. The Advancement Chair keeps the council list.
  3. Contact the counsellor yourself. Bring a buddy to every meeting with them — that is a Youth Protection rule, not a suggestion.
  4. Finish the requirements as written. Not more, not less.
  5. The counsellor signs the blue card. Keep your copy forever. People need theirs decades later.

Twenty-one merit badges are required for Eagle, fourteen of them from the Eagle-required list. Summer camp is where most Scouts knock out five or six in a week.

Scoutmaster conferences and boards of review

A Scoutmaster conference is a conversation, not a test. Ask for one when you have finished everything else for the rank. A board of review comes after, with three or more committee members — they are checking that you did the work and that the troop is serving you well. They are not allowed to re-test you on requirements.

Wear your field uniform to both. Bring your handbook. Boards of review are usually held on the first Tuesday of the month; ask the Advancement Chair to be put on the list.

Service hours and positions of responsibility

Star, Life and Eagle each need service hours and a stretch of time in a position of responsibility. Log your service the night you do it — reconstructing it a year later is miserable and usually costs you hours you actually earned. Eagle projects do not count toward the Eagle service hour requirement; they are separate.

Fastest route to First Class

  • Camp every month, without skipping
  • Bring your handbook every single time
  • Ask a Troop Guide to sign the same night you do it
  • Do the swimming requirement at summer camp
  • Cook for your patrol at least twice
